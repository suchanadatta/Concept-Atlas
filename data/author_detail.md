# 📚 17th & 18th Century Philosophers and Writers

A curated reference guide to key figures in early modern philosophy, theology, and feminist thought.

---

### Mary Astell (1666–1731)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English philosopher and early feminist writer from Newcastle. 
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *A Serious Proposal to the Ladies* — Argued for a women's academic retreat.
    * *Some Reflections upon Marriage* — Critiqued the subordination of wives.
* **Context:** Corresponded with John Norris on the love of God and was a High Church Tory polemicist.

### James Balfour of Pilrig (1705–1795)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Scottish advocate and later Professor of Moral Philosophy and Public Law at Edinburgh.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Delineation of the Nature and Obligation of Morality (1753)* — Defended a rationalist, religiously grounded morality against David Hume's sentimentalism.

### George Berkeley (1685–1753)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Irish philosopher, educated at Trinity College Dublin, and later Bishop of Cloyne.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * **Immaterialism:** Best known for the philosophical stance *"to be is to be perceived"* (*esse est percipi*) outlined in *Principles* and *Three Dialogues*.
    * **Other Works:** Wrote extensively on vision, mathematics (*The Analyst*), and tar-water (*Siris*).

### Elizabeth Burnet (1661–1709)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English religious writer and philanthropist; third wife of Bishop Gilbert Burnet.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *A Method of Devotion (1708)* — Author of this influential devotional text.
* **Context:** Corresponded directly with John Locke on philosophy and theology, focusing notably on Shaftesbury and moral questions.

### Joseph Butler (1692–1752)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English Anglican bishop (Bristol, then Durham) and moral philosopher.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Fifteen Sermons (1726)* — Offered an influential account of conscience, self-love, and benevolence.
    * *The Analogy of Religion (1736)* — Written as a direct answer to the deists.

### Archibald Campbell (1691–1756)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Scottish minister and Professor of Ecclesiastical History at St Andrews.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Enquiry into the Original of Moral Virtue (1733)* — Attacked Mandeville and argued that self-love underlies virtue.
* **Context:** Faced heresy charges within the Church of Scotland due to his views.

### Sarah Chapone (1699–1764)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English writer (née Kirkham) and close friend of Samuel Richardson and Mary Delany.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Hardships of the English Laws in Relation to Wives (1735)* — A pointed, landmark critique of coverture and the legal powerlessness of married women.

### Lady Mary Chudleigh (1656–1710)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English poet and essayist from Devon.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *The Ladies Defence (1701)* — A verse dialogue rebutting a sermon on wifely obedience.
    * *Poems and Essays upon Several Subjects* — Advocated strongly for women's learning and Stoic self-command.

### Samuel Clarke (1675–1729)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English Anglican clergyman and leading Newtonian philosopher.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * **Boyle Lectures:** Provided a cosmological argument for God and a rationalist ethics of "fitness".
    * **Debates:** Argued with Dodwell/Collins on the soul/liberty, and famously with Leibniz on space and time. Accused of Arianism over the Trinity.

### Catharine Trotter Cockburn (1679–1749)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English playwright, novelist, and philosopher.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * **Drama:** Early success on the London stage (e.g., *The Unhappy Penitent*).
    * **Philosophy:** Wrote a celebrated *Defence of Locke's Essay* and later works defending Clarke's moral rationalism. Her collected *Works* were published by Thomas Birch in 1751.

### Ralph Cudworth (1617–1888)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English theologian, Master of Christ's College, and leading Cambridge Platonist. Father of Damaris Masham.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *The True Intellectual System of the Universe (1678)* — Attacked atheism and materialism.
    * *Treatise concerning Eternal and Immutable Morality* — Posthumous work defending the objectivity of moral truths.

### Judith Drake (fl. 1696–1723)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English writer and medical practitioner.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *An Essay in Defence of the Female Sex (1696)* — A witty argument asserting that women's apparent inferiority stems from education, not nature (long misattributed to Mary Astell).

### David Fordyce (1711–1751)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Scottish philosopher and Professor of Moral Philosophy at Marischal College, Aberdeen.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Elements of Moral Philosophy* — A widely used textbook heavily influenced by Francis Hutcheson.
* **Context:** Tragically drowned in a shipwreck off the Dutch coast in 1751.

### Richard Gwinnett (d. 1717)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Gloucestershire gentleman and long-standing suitor of the poet Elizabeth Thomas.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *The Honourable Lovers / Pylades and Corinna (1731–32)* — A posthumously published collection of the couple's long romantic correspondence. He passed away before they could marry.

### David Hume (1711–1776)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Scottish philosopher, historian, and essayist; a central figure of the Scottish Enlightenment.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *A Treatise of Human Nature* & *Enquiries* — Developed an empiricist account of causation, human passions, and morality.
    * **Religion:** Sharp skeptic who challenged traditional religious views in *Natural History of Religion* and *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*.

### Francis Hutcheson (1694–1746)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Ulster-born philosopher and Professor of Moral Philosophy at Glasgow. Widely regarded as the "father of the Scottish Enlightenment."
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * **Moral Sense Theory:** Developed the theory of a "moral sense" and benevolence against the egoism of Hobbes and Mandeville.
    * **Legacy:** Mentored and taught Adam Smith.

### Thomas Johnson (d. 1737)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English cleric and Cambridge scholar (Magdalene College).
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Essay on Moral Obligation (1731)* — Intervened in the contemporary debate on whether moral duties rest on the will of God or the nature of things, siding firmly with the voluntarist view.

### Johnston (Identity Uncertain)
* **Gender:** `Male (Probably)`
* **Profile:** Author of *The Eternal Obligation of Natural Religion (1732)*.
* **Context:** Provided a contribution to the rationalist debates sparked by Samuel Clarke and his critics. Full identity remains unestablished; verify against source editions before relying heavily on this entry.

### William King (1650–1729)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Irish Anglican clergyman and Archbishop of Dublin.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *De Origine Mali (1702)* — Offered a classic theodicy explaining how evil is compatible with a benevolent God. It was later translated with extensive annotations by Edmund Law as *An Essay on the Origin of Evil (1731)*.

### Edmund Law (1703–1787)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English cleric and philosopher; Master of Peterhouse and later Bishop of Carlisle.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Considerations on the Theory of Religion (1745)* — Argued for progressive revelation. Associated heavily with Lockean, latitudinarian theology and translated William King's *Origin of Evil*.

### John Locke (1632–1704)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English philosopher and physician; universally recognized as the founder of British empiricism.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, *Two Treatises of Government*, and *Some Thoughts concerning Education* — Enormously influential texts that shaped modern political and psychological thought.
    * **Network:** Voluminous correspondence network including Damaris Masham, Elizabeth Burnet, and Catharine Trotter Cockburn.

### Bernard Mandeville (1670–1733)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Dutch-born physician and satirist who settled in London.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *The Fable of the Bees* — Provoked fierce controversy with his tagline *"private vices, publick benefits"*, arguing that commercial prosperity is driven by self-interest and vice. Also wrote on hypochondria and honor.

### Damaris Cudworth Masham (1659–1708)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English philosopher, daughter of Ralph Cudworth, and close friend of John Locke (who lived at her estate, Oates).
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *A Discourse concerning the Love of God (1696)* — Written as an answer to John Norris and Mary Astell.
    * *Occasional Thoughts (1705)* — Strongly argued for women's education. Corresponded directly with Leibniz.

### Susanna Newcome (1685–1763)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English theological writer and wife of John Newcome (Master of St John's College, Cambridge).
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Enquiry into the Evidence of the Christian Religion (1728)* — Anonymously published defense of Christianity on rational grounds. Her sacrament pieces relate to the controversy over Hoadly's *Plain Account*.

### John Norris (1657–1711)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English clergyman, philosopher, and rector of Bemerton; the leading English disciple of Nicolas Malebranche.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * **Theology:** Wrote on divine love, the ideal world, and human happiness. He famously engaged in correspondence with Mary Astell and published critiques of John Locke.

### Alexander Pope (1688–1744)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English poet and satirist; the leading literary figure of the Augustan age.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *An Essay on Man (1733–34)* — Philosophical poem influenced by Bolingbroke seeking to *"vindicate the ways of God to man"*. It was actively defended by Warburton against charges of fatalism.

### Thomas Rutherforth (1712–1771)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English cleric and Regius Professor of Divinity at Cambridge.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Essay on the Nature and Obligations of Virtue (1744)* — Criticized moral-sense theory in favor of a self-interested, theological basis for virtue. His *Institutes of Natural Law* were lectures expanding on Grotius.

### Anthony Ashley Cooper, 3rd Earl of Shaftesbury (1671–1713)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English philosopher and moralist; tutored during his childhood by John Locke.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Characteristicks of Men, Manners, Opinions, Times* — Introduced the concept of the "moral sense" and pioneered an aesthetic, benevolent approach to ethics. His *Several Letters (1716)* were addressed to a young Oxford student.

### Adam Smith (1723–1790)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Scottish philosopher and economist; student of Francis Hutcheson and close friend of David Hume.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *The Theory of Moral Sentiments (1759)* — Grounded human morality in sympathy and the concept of the "impartial spectator." 
    * *The Wealth of Nations (1776)* — Later authored this foundational text of modern economics.

### Elizabeth Thomas (1675–1731)
* **Gender:** `Female`
* **Profile:** English poet and letter-writer known by the moniker "Corinna" (given to her by John Dryden).
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Miscellany Poems (1722)* — Included verse advocating for women's education.
* **Context:** Infamously satirized in Alexander Pope’s *Dunciad* after selling his private letters to the publisher Edmund Curll.

### George Turnbull (1698–1748)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** Scottish philosopher and regent at Marischal College, Aberdeen, where he taught Thomas Reid.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Principles of Moral Philosophy (1740)* — Applied the Newtonian experimental method to moral ethics in a Shaftesburian, providentialist spirit.

### William Warburton (1698–1779)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English literary critic, controversialist, and later Bishop of Gloucester.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *The Divine Legation of Moses* — Argued that the lack of a future state in Mosaic law proved its divine origin. Served as Alexander Pope's literary executor and defended his *Essay on Man*.

### Isaac Watts (1674–1748)
* **Gender:** `Male`
* **Profile:** English Nonconformist minister, theologian, and celebrated hymn-writer.
* **Key Contributions:** 
    * *Logick (1724)* & *The Improvement of the Mind* — Broadly Lockean in outlook, these works served as standard academic educational texts across the English-speaking world for over a century.
